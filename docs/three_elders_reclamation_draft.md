# The Age of the Three Elders — Reclamation of Jambhudweepa

Status: draft narrative treatment for author review. No existing event, character, epoch, map, game data or deployment is changed. The author's requested sequence is Asuran defeat first, avian defeat second, Vanaran ascendancy and eventual human inheritance. Specific scenes and titles below are proposed.

## Historical placement

The prehistoric Fang–Scale Wars, Vaishura's domestication and the Gondwana Intervention precede the reclamation. The ancient campaigns may begin in late Prehistoric Foundations and extend into early Deep Antiquity; their precise boundary and duration remain open. The Vanaran ascendancy must precede their Great Devolvement, which leads into the Jharwa migration. Do not equate the primordial rift at the Ganges Lava Sea with the later documented Dwarka Gate site. Do not compress geological time into one generation or settle the disputed taxonomy of prehistoric Vanaras. The Elders' continuity may be spiritual; exact lifespans remain unresolved.

## I. Lionman — the road of the sun

Lionman encounters scattered Vanaran communities living between corrupted forests and the open grasslands. His first attempt to gather them fails: the refugees cannot feed themselves outside the canopy, and his insistence on returning to the old settlements exposes their vulnerability. He learns to guide rather than command, establishing seasonal routes between clean springs, sheltered groves and safe grazing country. His followers become the first organized resistance capable of maintaining settlements outside the anomaly zones.

When Vaishura's vanguard advances, Lionman refuses to treat the imported cynodonts and gorgonopsids as inherently evil. He distinguishes the creatures from the coercive system that weaponizes them. The resistance breaks the vanguard's territorial hold; surviving animals are released, contained or integrated according to ecological risk. His victory makes the Vanaran communities a political force.

After the Asuran defeat, avian rulers refuse Vanaran access to the newly recovered interior. Lionman leads a second campaign to end avian political supremacy. His alliance with Owlman nearly breaks over the treatment of nesting communities. Lionman accepts protected refuges but insists that the central forests and plains can no longer be governed exclusively by avian kingdoms.

His final triumph is not permanent. When cooling fractures the canopy civilization, Lionman again guides scattered communities across the savanna, preserving the existing Great Devolvement account. His arc runs from protector to liberator to founder to guide of a declining civilization.

## II. Owlman — the divided forest

Owlman initially protects native avian settlements and Vanaran forest corridors alike. The Asuran intervention corrupts the ecology he adjudicates: ancestral territorial signals cease to be reliable, familiar prey becomes dangerous, and forest boundaries become unstable. He develops a practice of observing and marking safe passages, allowing displaced communities to travel without trespassing into occupied nests.

Owlman discovers that defeating the vanguard without healing the land would merely exchange one ruler for another. He coordinates with Ammonite Man to distinguish anomalous sites from natural habitat and with Lionman to restore forest corridors. During the struggle against the embodied Asuran administrators, he negotiates temporary cooperation with native avian defenders. Some avian factions decline; others join because their own territories are threatened.

The second war is Owlman's tragedy. His avian kinship and responsibility for the forest conflict with the Vanaran demand for sovereignty. He opposes the old avian rulers' exclusion of Vanarans but refuses to abandon ordinary avian communities. He brokers withdrawal into protected southern and maritime refuges after the defeat of avian political power. The surviving Sylvians, Silvanus, Nagaraptor and Cognitavi traditions are not erased; their precise territorial outcomes require review.

Owlman remains a boundary keeper rather than a Vanaran monarch. Later human clearing and Vedda expansion renew the dilemma and culminate in his already documented retreat into the Western Ghats.

## III. Ammonite Man — the memory of the sea

Ammonite Man remembers the biosphere before the Asuran intervention. In this proposed arc he compares the resonant signatures of natural waters, corrupted springs and dimensional disturbances, recognizing that the anomalies are not simply foreign armies but persistent injuries to the land. His early warnings fail because terrestrial societies cannot interpret his long, abyssal signals. Owlman helps translate them into signs that can be preserved and transmitted by Vanaran communities.

He leads the effort to isolate unstable anomaly zones and preserve pre-intervention ecological knowledge. The Elders recover enough habitable land to undermine Vaishura's vanguard without destroying all of its imported fauna. When the more humanized Asuran rulers seek to retain influence through intermediaries, he recognizes their surviving claims and refuses any agreement that would restore control over the island.

After the defeat of the Asuran occupation, he attempts to negotiate coexistence between avian and Vanaran powers. The negotiations fail to preserve avian supremacy, but they establish the possibility of protected maritime passages and refuges. He preserves the memories of defeated communities as well as victors.

As the Vanaran world declines, he becomes the enduring witness who later negotiates the Shell Pact with Elder Repa. The pact remains the first documented Jharwa settlement, not the first human settlement. He does not grant humanity ownership of every habitat.

## IV. The two wars

### War of Reclamation — against Asuran dominion

1. **The First Warning from the Deep:** Ammonite Man identifies the persistence of the anomalies after the Gondwana Intervention; Owlman observes their inland effects.
2. **The Broken Forest Paths:** Owlman establishes safe corridors for displaced forest communities.
3. **The Gathering under the Sun:** Lionman unites Vanaran refuges into a resistance capable of maintaining settlements.
4. **The Covenant of Three Domains:** The Elders agree to combine terrestrial protection, forest mediation and abyssal memory.
5. **The Fall of the Fang Vanguard:** The Elders and Vanaran resistance defeat Vaishura's expeditionary military system. Vaishura is the existing individual, not a second commander; his death circumstances and dates are not asserted. His personal presence at the final defeat is open.
6. **The War against the Asuran Courts:** The resistance defeats the remaining embodied, more humanlike Asuran authorities and their local claims. Daedrasura is a proposed major adversarial influence whose precise manifestation and presence must be reviewed against the disputed prehistoric Seedbearer tradition and later dossier. Do not assert his death or permanent destruction; later Naraka activity remains intact. No new role is assigned to Manjalaya, Sarishne, Gagdji, Kalyani or the Asura-Tainted Princess.
7. **The Closing of the Wounded Lands:** The Elders contain the remaining unstable zones and restore viable habitats. Anomalies persist at some sites; the later Shadow Pact and future Gate incidents remain possible.

### War of the Feathered Crowns — against avian supremacy

8. **The Dispute of the Reclaimed Forests:** Former co-belligerents dispute access to recovered habitats. Some avian rulers claim exclusive sovereignty; not all avian peoples agree.
9. **The Broken Accord:** Ammonite Man and Owlman attempt a settlement; rival territorial claims defeat it.
10. **The War of the Feathered Crowns:** Lionman leads the Vanaran coalition against the ruling avian powers. Owlman defends noncombatant communities and seeks a negotiated end.
11. **The Withdrawal of the Feathered Kingdoms:** Avian supremacy ends on the principal Vanaran territories. Surviving avian societies retain refuges, autonomy and cultural continuity; exact places and named rulers are open.
12. **The Vanaran Ascendancy:** Vanaran tree settlements, fire mastery, ecological stewardship and transmission of knowledge flourish on the reclaimed island. This is a proposed political culmination, not a new biological origin for simians.

### Inheritance and decline

13. **The Keepers of the Living Memory:** Vanaran custodians preserve the lessons of the Elders; the existing Jambanson and moon-seed traditions remain independently dated until reconciled.
14. **The Great Devolvement:** Existing event; climate and habitat fragmentation undermine the civilization. Lionman resumes his role as guide.
15. **The First Covenant of Beast and Human:** Surviving intelligent Vanaras pass fragments of nature-bound practice to older pre-Jharwa humans; Pashupati's court is a later institutional expression. Arrival route and date of that population are unknown.
16. **The Shell Pact and the Retreat of Owlman:** Existing migration events conclude the Elder cycle in different ways: Ammonite Man remains available to new arrivals; Owlman withdraws when the forest is again threatened.

## V. Proposed continuity rulings requiring review

- The second war is a defeat of avian political hegemony, not the extinction of birds or sentient dinosaurids. Some avian factions fight beside the Elders in the first war.
- Reclamation establishes ecological and political conditions favorable to simians; it does not imply humans descend directly from any named Vanaran species.
- Vaishura's vanguard is defeated without inventing his death date, a second Vaishura, or the order of his offices.
- Daedrasura's early participation is proposed, not a silent change to the dossier's Civilization Dawn first-appearance field. His local defeat is compatible with later activity.
- The existing Great Devolvement, Shell Pact, Shadow Pact and Owlman retreat remain unchanged. The exact position of the new campaigns relative to the prehistoric Jambanson cycle is unresolved.
- The proposed titles and scenes are new fiction. The Elders' personal motives, disagreements, defeat mechanisms, and protected refuges require authorial approval.
