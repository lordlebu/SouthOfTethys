# The Coalition and Its Aftermath
*A location-neutral register for the war remembered under Sudās*

## The war's central problem
The familiar title *Battle of Ten Kings* is not a reliable list of ten equally sovereign rulers. The war hymn presents named peoples, fighting groups, leaders and hostile designations in a rhetorically shaped account. Some names may overlap in allegiance or change their meaning between verses. The University therefore records **claims about participation** rather than inventing ten nation-state borders.

## Four layers of aftermath
**The rulers.** Victory or defeat changes claims to tribute, protection and passage. It does not necessarily extinguish every local authority under a defeated banner.

**The households.** Families may remain, move, seek sanctuary or renegotiate obligations with another court. An entire named people should not be declared a refugee population on the strength of one royal defeat.

**The skilled custodians.** Singers, metalworkers, ferrymen and fire-keepers can transfer techniques to new patrons. A craft may cross a border without the practitioner's original political affiliation surviving.

**The receiving communities.** Vedda, Naga and established Harappan communities possess their own histories. They may accept migrants, refuse them or exchange goods without becoming subordinate to the victor's genealogy.

## Tests for an actual historical merger
A valid merger claim should identify the earlier group, the receiving locality, the mechanism of entry, the intermediate evidence and the later outcome. A treaty is not the same as conquest; adoption is not the same as descent; a surviving title is not a surviving people.

| Proposed outcome | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Defeated house entered a Harappan ward | A dated ward record or independently supported local testimony |
| Ritual school became a civic office | Sequence of appointments, teaching records or legal formulae |
| Caravan route became a canal or rail corridor | Route evidence and intermediate administrative records |
| Political group retained independence | Separate court, treaty, tribute or boundary testimony |
| Name disappeared but practice survived | Dated chain of practices, not a single resemblance |

## Outstanding enquiries
The number and identity of opposing rulers; the relationship between the coalition and the earlier river crossing; independent evidence from those defeated; which people remained outside Harappan jurisdiction; and whether any documented post-war settlement precedes Lothal's established founding.

The University will not close these questions by distributing ten names across ten invented kingdoms.
