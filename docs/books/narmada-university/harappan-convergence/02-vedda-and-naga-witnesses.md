# Kashyapa, Vedda Memory and the Witnesses of the Naga Courts
*On the danger of forcing unlike records into one family tree*

## The Vedda origin-name
Kashyapa remains a remote figure of Vedda oral memory in the surviving archive. His place is the Era of Human Migrations, and the remembered role is the origin of the Vedda lineage. No temple biography, complete reign, written testament or continuous genealogy has survived. The University cannot turn an origin-name into an eyewitness of every later kingdom.

The later Kāśyapa name known through hymn classifications might represent a singer or a line of singers. Whether it names the **same Kashyapa** is unresolved. On present evidence, the University separates the Vedda ancestral account from the later scholarly attribution, while allowing that travellers may have repeated the name.

## Vedda settlements and later migrants
Vedda groups were neither empty-land predecessors nor an unnamed substrate waiting for a new tribe to arrive. Their territories, ford memories and ritual custodianships formed part of the political environment encountered by later households. The Vedda Ford is a known place of memory. Connections between its documented local history and any specific Sudās-period expedition require independent testimony.

Hospitality, raiding, negotiation and joint travel may all have occurred in different seasons. A Vedda guide could have carried a caravan into Harappan markets without surrendering his clan's independence.

## The Naga courts
The Naga Clan and Naga-Raja belong to existing records. The court world of the Naga-Raja, maritime conflict, the Black Lotus Siege and contact with Lothal are part of Civilization Dawn. The Naga Clan has its own account of origins, diplomacy, metalwork and religious observance. Neither the Anu nor the Bhṛgus may be installed as its hereditary ancestor without a separate record.

The University should collect Naga court tablets and the testimony of coastal pilots before assigning any newly studied people to a Naga dynasty. A trading treaty would demonstrate contact, not descent.

## The question of disputed identifications
Inscriptions bearing a similar name may record a guest, a prince, a village or an office. The scholar who encounters such a name must document its script, findspot, custody and date before arguing identity.

A particularly hazardous error would be to make every mention of Kashyapa an account of one long-lived traveller. More responsible catalogues preserve variant testimonies, date their transmission and state when identity is uncertain.

## A proposed sequence of encounters
1. Independent Vedda and Naga populations occupy and name their own landscapes.
2. Later migrating houses negotiate crossing, refuge, pasture and exchange with local custodians.
3. Rival coalitions rearrange frontier alliances.
4. During Civilization Dawn, some descendants or institutional heirs appear in the trade records of Harappan towns while other communities remain outside their jurisdiction.
5. In the Age of Machinery, local memory is recovered through university fieldwork, district records and surviving practices rather than a single uninterrupted family chart.

This sequence is a research framework, not a newly attested set of events.
