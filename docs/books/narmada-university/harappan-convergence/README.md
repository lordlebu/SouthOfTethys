# The Harappan Convergence Chronicles
*Collected papers of the Narmada University Collegium of Historical Inquiry*

This collection follows the meeting of migratory houses, older settlements, riverine courts and coastal polities; their conflicts during the late migration generations; their varying incorporation into the Harappan city-world; and the survival of their languages, trades, political memories and institutions into the Age of Machinery.

The term **convergence** does not mean that the peoples became one bloodline or that their kingdoms were all absorbed by force. It describes several processes—treaty, adoption, apprenticeship, displacement, marriage, trade, conquest, civic participation and continued independence—which did not occur simultaneously.

## University method
A name may signify a person, house, political people, ritual school, title, adversarial designation or narrative symbol. The same name in different centuries does not prove the same person. Witnesses separated by centuries are not forced into invented meetings.

The University records four grades of claim: **attested in the surviving archive**; **traditional attribution**; **working reconstruction**; and **unresolved**. These grades appear where an identification materially affects the account. The body of each chronicle is written as history rather than as a catalogue of disclaimers.

No revised ancient borders, genealogies or fixed dates are presumed beyond the surviving chronology. A royal house's participation in Harappan exchange does not prove that it founded Harappa.

## Archives consulted
- The established epoch register, Lothal records and the pre-Shattering chart.
- The older Narmada University ancestral transmission dossiers.
- A separate comparative register of Rigvedic passages and later attributions, held in [the scholarly concordance](10-scholarly-concordance.md).

These volumes are preliminary research manuscripts. They do not amend the structured historical register.
