# Concordance of the Migration Peoples with the South of Tethys World
*The Narmada University commission's institutional and political crosswalk*

## Principle
This concordance anchors every newly introduced people to the **existing South of Tethys geography and institutional history**. It separates (1) a local entity already present in the structured archive, (2) a historical relationship adopted for the Narmada manuscripts, and (3) an interpretive link proposed for future review. The third category is not a newly discovered event.

The migration peoples do not replace the Vedda, Naga, Harappan, Kia, Tuli, Silvershore or Yaksha populations. A people may enter another people's territory by treaty, marriage, service, trade or apprenticeship while retaining its own name.

## I. The western harbour: Yadu, North Dwarka and Lothal
The Yadus were a mixed **Vedda–Naga–Harappan** people who founded **North Dwarka** on the Saraswati-delta coast. Their coastal hegemony preceded the rise of Kavik's Lothal. The specific North Dwarka is the harbour recorded as `database/settlements/settlement_dwarka.json`, near the submerged Dwarka Gate; it is **not** the separately recorded `database/places/place_dwarka_dwarkadhish.json`.

The Yadu harbour belonged to an older political order with which the Mask Family's merchants and negotiators could interact. **Kavik**, whose migration from Mohenjo-daro and founding of Lothal around 3000 BCE are recorded in `database/events/event_founding_lothal.json`, remains the founder of Lothal. The Yadus are **not** made its founders. The later northern harbour became a seat of the **Vayu principality** under the Vayu & Dwarkadhish Coral Monarchies; the change of government from the earlier Yadu hegemony has not yet been documented.

**Firm connection:** Yadu foundation of North Dwarka, earlier hegemony, and subsequent contact with the Mask Family in the same coastal world (adopted Narmada history).

**Unresolved:** the first Yadu ruler, exact relationship to the Mask Family, whether hegemony ended by conflict or treaty, and the sequence leading to the Vayu principality.

## II. The northeastern settlement: Bharata and Hastinapura
At the **opening of Civilization Dawn**, the **Bharatas, a Naga people**, established a settlement at Hastinapura. The structured place entry `database/places/place_hastinapura.json` records the **later post-Harappan town** in the northeastern plain. This later description does not erase its earlier Naga–Bharata foundation in the Narmada chronicle.

The Bharata political name also occurs in the comparative Sudās traditions. Those songs do not establish that Sudās founded Hastinapura or ruled every Naga court. The Tṛtsus, associated with the Sudās court, should be studied as a possible **court-service and patronage layer** within the broader Bharata history, rather than given an invented separate capital.

**Firm connection:** Naga Bharata foundation at Hastinapura at Civilization Dawn's beginning (adopted Narmada history); later northeastern town (structured archive).

**Unresolved:** exact relation to Sudās and Tṛtsu, first local ruler, and continuity of dynastic government.

## III. The eastern healing houses: Bhṛgu, Urva, Aurva and Chandraketugarh
The Bhṛgu houses did not follow one ruler or one route. Their eastern teaching branch remembers the ṛṣis **Urva and Aurva**; later inheritors reached the existing **Vedda apothecary institutions of Chandraketugarh**. The city appears in `database/places/place_chandraketugarh.json` as an island city under the **Vanga Maritime Federation**, with Vedda apothecary guilds feuding with **Daedrasura's cultists** over Garudasaur blood. The conflict is also recorded in `docs/books/narmada-university/03-daedrasura-inheritance.md`.

The apothecaries' expression **Garudasura moss** is another name for the **existing Garudasaur blood material**; it does not designate a separate moss species. Urva and Aurva's teaching tradition is a key part of the acquisition, custody and preparation of that material. Vedda practitioners retain their independent healing knowledge; the Bhṛgu line contributes a specialized transmission rather than founding the city or replacing its guilds.

The Vanga Federation's documented origins in **Tuli pearl-divers, delta merchants and eastern seafarers** are not overwritten by the arrival of a Bhṛgu-descended school. A city can host Vedda guilds, Bhṛgu custodians and Vanga maritime government simultaneously.

**Firm connection:** Bhṛgu–Urva–Aurva teaching succession and its decisive contribution to the Vedda Garudasaur-material tradition (adopted Narmada history); Chandraketugarh, Vanga jurisdiction and Daedrasura dispute (structured and older manuscript records).

**Unresolved:** generations between the ṛṣis and the industrial city; exact methods and the origin of the material's guild nickname.

## IV. The southern passage: Jharwa, Kia, Agasthya and Yaksha courts
The **Jharwa** had travelled into the southern **Yaksha** lands before **Agasthya**, a **Kia ṛṣi**, journeyed south in the Age of Machinery. The culture register `database/cultures.json` identifies the Yaksha south as Kubera's territory and records a southern railway through it. The Kia have an independent delta heritage and an established later role among the artisans of the Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union.

Agasthya's **humanization** of Yaksha society is **cultural and institutional**: teaching, civic procedure, recordkeeping, settlement administration and negotiated interaction with human polities. It is not a change of species or a conquest. Yaksha rulers retain sovereignty, and their institutions can adopt or reject individual practices.

The **Realm of Prince Vijaya** also appears in the southern-tip political register, but its culture and relationship to the Yakshas remain explicitly **unknown** in `database/factions/faction_realm_of_prince_vijaya.json`. Agasthya's journey does not silently resolve that open political question.

**Firm connection:** Jharwa earlier passage; Agasthya's later Kia cultural influence; independent Yaksha rule (adopted Narmada history supported by established geographic and cultural anchors).

**Unresolved:** Agasthya's itinerary, named Yaksha collaborators and any institutional relationship to Prince Vijaya's realm.

The **Hyrkanian & Tushara Rail-Khanates** already have a documented Tushara and northern-pass origin in `database/factions/faction_hyrkanian_tushara_rail_khanates.json`. A resemblance between ancient caravan guards and industrial rail escorts does not prove Druhyu descent.

The **Shwetavahana Riverine League** explicitly descends from **Silvershore river-lords and Indus canal-masters** (`database/factions/faction_shwetavahana_riverine_league.json`). A Pūru or Anu river custom could have been borrowed, but the League must not be relabelled a Pūru or Anu state without a surviving intermediate record.

## VI. Lothal as the meeting place, not the source of all peoples
The structured event graph remains intact:

1. The **Silvershore War** concerns Kavik's debt refusal and the raid on Lothal docks.
2. Kavik's **Founding of Lothal** establishes his tower and Harappan ascendancy in the delta.
3. The **Black Lotus Siege** involves Kavik and Naga-Raja.
4. The **Stone Pact** binds Shaashak and Khadi in a political marriage and peace with Kia.
5. The **Tendua Crisis** leads to Kavik's death and a matriarchal council.

The graph's exact partial order is controlled by the event files; the numbering here identifies the principal narrative sequence, not newly assigned dates.

Yadu traders and Naga–Bharata envoys could have encountered Lothal, but the event records do not name them. The **Naga-Raja** of the Black Lotus Siege must not be identified as a Bharata king by default. The **Stone Pact** belongs to the Mask Family and Kia, not to an unrecorded Tṛtsu marriage. The **Silvershore** remain a separate riverine lineage.

## VII. The Age of Machinery: what actually survives
The existing industrial polities demonstrate that mixed societies can preserve different origins without becoming a single lineage:

| Later polity or locality | Established inheritance | New Narmada historical layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union** | Mask Family, Harappan stone-engineers, urbanised Kia artisans | Yadu coastal contacts and other migrant workshop influences may be researched, but are not substitute founders |
| **North Dwarka / Vayu principality** | Late Vayu coastal seat near submerged Gate | Earlier Yadu foundation and pre-Lothal hegemony |
| **Hastinapura** | Post-Harappan northeastern town | Bharata Naga foundation at beginning of Civilization Dawn |
| **Chandraketugarh / Vanga Maritime Federation** | Vedda apothecary city within Tuli-merchant maritime polity | Bhṛgu–Urva–Aurva custody of Garudasaur blood, called Garudasura moss |
| **Yaksha south** | Kubera's Yaksha territory; southern rail | Agasthya's Kia-led cultural and institutional exchange after earlier Jharwa passage |
| **Shwetavahana Riverine League** | Silvershore descendants and Indus canal-masters | Comparative study of older crossing practices; no new ethnic origin |
| **Hyrkanian & Tushara Rail-Khanates** | Tushara northern-pass and steppe industrial traditions | Comparative study of mobile escorts; no Druhyu descent claim |
| **Kingdom of Pragjyotisha** | Eastern Harappan remnant kingdom | No assignment of Pūru, Anu or Bharata descent; relation to Union still open |
| **Realm of Prince Vijaya** | Southern-tip kingdom; culture unknown | No invented Yadu, Kia or Yaksha ancestry |
| **Narmada Academic Republic** | Established scholarly polity | The University records these rival histories; documentation is not proof of a single founding people |

## VIII. Evidence gates
The following links are **part of the adopted Narmada narrative**: Yadu–North Dwarka; Bharata–Hastinapura; Urva–Aurva–Chandraketugarh; Agasthya–Yaksha cultural change. Their **structured archive anchors** are cited above, and draft event records now encode all four connections: `event_yadu_foundation_north_dwarka`, `event_bharata_settlement_hastinapura`, `event_bhrgu_teaching_enters_vedda_guilds` and `event_agasthya_yaksha_southern_exchange`. The first two belong to Civilization Dawn, the last to the Age of Machinery, and the Bhṛgu transmission event remains undated. The settlement state histories still record only the later industrial geography. See the [epoch register](23-character-and-tribal-epoch-register.md).

Other proposed links—Pūru river charters, Anu market settlement, Druhyu caravan ancestry, Turvaśa maritime households, and fixed homelands for smaller coalition names—remain **investigative hypotheses**. A future author must find or explicitly create a justified historical event before describing any of them as settled history.

The University's task is not to turn every name into an ancestor of an industrial nation. It is to preserve **how distinct peoples could meet, contend, intermarry and exchange knowledge while the established world retained its own history**.
