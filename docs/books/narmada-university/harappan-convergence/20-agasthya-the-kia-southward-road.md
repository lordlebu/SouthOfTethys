# Agasthya and the Kia Southward Road
*An Age of Machinery journey into the lands of the Yakshas*

## The south before the traveller
The southern lands were not empty. The University's cultural register places **Kubera's Yakshas** in the south of the island and records a southern railway passing through their country. Older Jharwa journeys had reached Yaksha lands before the Kia traveller of this chronicle. The earlier arrivals did not extinguish Yaksha sovereignty.

## The Kia inheritance
The Kia belong to the delta's own people. Their rafts, marsh knowledge and artisan traditions helped shape the Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union. The Stone Pact established a political peace between Kavik's house and Kia through Shaashak and Khadi. That ancient settlement did not require the Kia to remain permanently confined to the delta.

In the Age of Machinery, **Agasthya** is introduced as a **Kia ṛṣi and travelling investigator**, one who carries river knowledge into the south. This is a new worldbuilding figure and must not be mistaken for a previously attested database character or an automatically dated Rigvedic biography.

## The southward passage
Agasthya travels through a landscape already crossed by Jharwa wayfarers and inhabited by Yaksha communities. His task is not conquest or the discovery of an unpeopled land. He investigates changes in water, plants, embankments and routes where the southern rail and older paths meet.

The Kia experience of flood, reed and river-whale movement gives him useful questions but not automatic authority. Yaksha custodians know their own soils, boundaries and obligations. A sound chronicle of Agasthya's journey must allow those custodians to refuse a crossing, demand a witness or teach him what the delta cannot.

## Meeting older Jharwa memories
Agasthya may find shelters, names and route practices associated with earlier Jharwa passage. Their survival would be a matter for field investigation. It would not prove that Jharwa and Kia travelled together, shared one origin or arrived in a single migration.

His comparison of the older Jharwa path with the industrial rail corridor is especially valuable: one road may carry a memory without preserving the political authority of those who first used it.

## A university dossier
The commission proposes that Agasthya's southern notes be studied as an **Age of Machinery field tradition**. Their subjects include railway waterworks, marsh-borne medicine, Yaksha territorial agreements and the risks of treating a survey line as ownership.

No existing archive yet supplies his departure year, itinerary, companions, meeting with Kubera or a named Yaksha settlement. Those details should be developed in a later field journal rather than presented here as recovered fact.

## Commission's finding
**Adopted worldbuilding direction:** Agasthya is Kia and journeys south during the Age of Machinery, after older Jharwa movement into Yaksha lands.

**Established anchors:** Kia delta culture and its contribution to the Union; southern Yaksha homeland and railway.

**Open questions:** exact route, local hosts, institutional purpose and dated field notes. The journey connects cultures through inquiry and negotiation rather than making the south a Kia colony.
