# The Interwoven Peoples: A Late Migration Register
*The last layer of affiliations before the Age of Machinery*

## How the houses became neighbours
Between the Vedda and Naga movements and the rise of industrial institutions, communities acquired additional identities through marriage, patronage, adopted rites, apprenticeship, trade and local political allegiance. The University distinguishes **a people** from **a court**, **a lineage**, **a craft school** and **a place**. No settlement is treated as the ancestral property of everyone who later lived there.

## The Naga and Vedda intervals
The Naga Clan's established record includes Vedda and Indus migrant origins and Takshak-linked traditions. This does not make every newly named house a subdivision of that clan. Yadu and Bharata may be **associated with Naga communities in different regions and generations** without sharing a single sovereign court.

The Vedda Kashyapa memory remains an earlier oral origin tradition. Later eastern Vedda apothecaries retain their own institutional inheritance even when a Bhṛgu-descended school works beside them. Exchange must not be rewritten as the replacement of an older people.

## The earlier western hegemony
**North Dwarka** was founded by the Yadus, a Vedda–Naga–Harappan people, and became a coastal hegemony before Lothal's rise. This is the northern harbour near the submerged Dwarka Gate, not the separate Dwarkadhish's Dwarka. The later Vayu principality belongs to a different political phase. The relationship to the Mask Family is preserved without inventing named marriages.

## Two terminal settlements
**Hastinapura** was settled by the Bharatas, a Naga people, at the beginning of Civilization Dawn. The post-Harappan designation describes its later recorded urban condition and does not negate the earlier foundation.

**Chandraketugarh** remains an eastern island city of Vedda apothecary guilds under Vanga jurisdiction. Urva and Aurva's Bhṛgu teaching tradition is central to the acquisition and preservation of Garudasaur blood, called Garudasura moss in the guild vocabulary; later descendants reach the established city. The name does not introduce a separate plant.

## Southern continuity
Agasthya's Kia expedition takes place in the Age of Machinery and helps humanize Yaksha institutions through cultural exchange without physically transforming the Yakshas or removing their rulers. Earlier Jharwa passage is a distinct tradition. The southern rail belongs to the later industrial geography, not the ancient migration route by default.

## The industrial threshold
The Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union remains rooted in Harappan stone-engineers, Mask Family history and urbanised Kia artisans. Chandraketugarh belongs to the Vanga Maritime Federation's world; Hastinapura lies on the northeastern plain; the Yaksha south is not a dependency of either. The later map shows a plural world, not a single empire inheriting all migration peoples.

## Commission's final classification
**Established local anchors:** Mask Family at Mohenjo-daro and Lothal; Kia delta culture and Stone Pact; Vedda Kashyapa memory; Naga Clan record; Hastinapura's late town; Chandraketugarh's Vedda guilds; Yaksha south; industrial Union.

**Adopted narrative additions:** Yadu foundation of North Dwarka and its pre-Lothal hegemony, with Mask affiliation; Bharata Naga foundation of Hastinapura at Civilization Dawn's opening; Urva and Aurva's crucial Garudasaur blood custody within Vedda guild history; Agasthya's Kia journey and cultural humanization of sovereign Yaksha communities.

**Unproven transfers:** specific marriages, ancient founding dates, exact routes, continuous biological genealogies, named intermediate guild charters, and claims of descent for other coalition peoples.

The final layers of contact are now represented in the University's chronicles. Their unresolved documentary seams remain visible so later scholarship can strengthen them without overturning the established world.
