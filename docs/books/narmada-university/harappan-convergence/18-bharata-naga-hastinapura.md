# The Bharata Naga Settlement at Hastinapura
*Foundation at the beginning of Civilization Dawn*

## The first settlement
At the **beginning of Civilization Dawn**, the **Bharatas, a Naga people**, established their settlement at Hastinapura on Jambhudweepa's northeastern plain. Their arrival belongs to the early history of the town, not merely to a late post-Harappan political association. The community settled among a landscape with its own inhabitants and obligations; the surviving account does not require the removal of those earlier residents.

## Bharata and the Naga world
The Bharatas formed a distinct Naga people. Their settlement did not make every Naga clan a Bharata or place all Naga courts under Hastinapura. Local alliances, household obligations and political institutions could bind the new settlement to other Naga communities while leaving them independent.

The comparative songs concerning Sudās and the Bharatas are valuable to the University's study of the name. They do not independently date the founding of this South of Tethys settlement or supply its first ruler.

## A town across the eras
Hastinapura's beginning in Civilization Dawn and its later description as a **post-Harappan town** are compatible: the latter describes its subsequent urban condition, not its first habitation. The later Age of Machinery atlas fixes the town on the northeastern plain, without establishing that its borders or government remained unchanged throughout.

## Relations with Harappa
The Bharata settlement could participate in trade, diplomacy and conflict with Harappan cities without becoming a Harappan colony. Imported objects would indicate exchange, not the replacement of Naga identity. The University's future work must trace any specific legal or craft inheritance through intermediate records.

## University finding
**Established in the present Narmada chronicle:** Bharatas are a Naga people; their Hastinapura settlement begins at the opening of Civilization Dawn.

**Open:** named founding ruler, exact generation within that opening period, relationships to Sudās and Tṛtsu, and the settlement's subsequent political succession.
