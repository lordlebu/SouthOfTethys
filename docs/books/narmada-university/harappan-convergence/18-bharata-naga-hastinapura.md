# The Bharata Houses of Hastinapura
*An inquiry into a Naga political settlement of the northeastern plain*

## The place before the title
Hastinapura is recorded in the later atlas as a **post-Harappan town on Jambhudweepa's northeastern plain**. The surviving place entry fixes its Age of Machinery position but does not date its founding or establish the identity of its earliest inhabitants. The University must therefore distinguish the town's attested late existence from its reconstructed earlier settlement.

## Bharata as a Naga association
The University's new account proposes that **Bharata** came to name a Naga political association settled in the vicinity of Hastinapura. The name could have been retained by allied households, a military retinue or a ruling assembly. It need not imply that every Naga court descended from Bharata or that all inhabitants of Hastinapura were Bharata.

In the comparative war songs, Bharata and Tṛtsu occur in traditions concerning Sudās. The later Naga–Hastinapura identification is an **in-world settlement history**, not a statement that those songs explicitly describe this town.

## The northeastern settlement
A plausible early sequence begins with Naga households negotiating grazing, ferry and fortified residence rights with existing inhabitants. Their assembly might have adopted the Bharata name as a political compact, while other Naga courts continued under different rulers. The University has not recovered a founding charter, a named first Bharata ruler or a date for the first settlement.

The town's post-Harappan designation is important. It should not be transformed into a royal capital existing at every earlier migration date merely to connect it to Sudās.

## Relations with the Harappan world
A Naga–Bharata settlement could have traded with Harappan markets while remaining politically independent. A Harappan craft or writing convention found in a later town would not establish Harappan conquest. Likewise, a Bharata treaty name in a future record would not automatically prove descent from Sudās.

The distinction between Naga Clan lineage, a Bharata political association and Hastinapura's wider population must remain visible. A place can host several cultures without becoming identical to one.

## The Age of Machinery
The late map preserves Hastinapura's location. Its councils, households and political memory may have retained an older Bharata designation, but no structured faction entry yet confirms that continuity. Industrial roads or rail lines through the northeastern plain would have negotiated with the town as it then existed, not with a timeless ancestral kingdom.

## Commission's finding
**Adopted worldbuilding direction:** a Bharata-associated Naga settlement near Hastinapura, growing into a post-Harappan town.

**Open questions:** foundation generation, earlier local population, names of rulers, ties to Sudās, and the exact relation between the settlement and other Naga courts. The Age of Machinery location is attested; earlier boundaries are not.
