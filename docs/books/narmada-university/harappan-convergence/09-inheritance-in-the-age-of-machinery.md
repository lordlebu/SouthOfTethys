# The Inheritance of Harappa in the Age of Machinery
*From oath, workshop and ferry to patent, foundry and rail*

## I. The city becomes a guild nation
The Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union is a documented nation of foundries, clockwork factories, copper solar-coil workshops, patents, ledgers and civic councils, centred upon Lothal. Its known historical inheritance is Harappan, Mask Family and urbanised Kia. Those foundations are not replaced by speculative migrant pedigrees.

The surviving question is what other techniques and institutions entered its stream before the age of machines. A teaching lineage that shared a method of securing a hearth might leave a trace in foundry practice, but the resemblance requires records before attribution.

## II. Four chains of transmission
**River rights to canal jurisdiction.** Early disputes over ford passage and water access may be comparable with the later rules of canal authorities, toll houses and the Shwetavahana Riverine League. An institutional chain must be documented rather than presumed.

**Witness songs to legal registers.** Traditions of oath recitation and itinerant testimony may resemble the city's written contracts and the Union's patent registries. The shift in record medium is not proof of a single ancestral school.

**Travelling makers to factories.** Workshops transmitted skills by apprenticeships across peoples. Industrial metallurgy may preserve several older craft systems, some of which could have passed through Bhṛgu houses. Kia and Harappan contributions remain independently attested.

**Caravan routes to rail corridors.** Earlier frontier bargains about shelter, cargo and escort may have successors in the Hyrkanian and Tushara Rail-Khanates, where coal and iron travel by armoured trains. A continuous route and the identity of its original custodians remain open to archaeological study.

## III. The plurality of the industrial age
The Narmada Academic Republic oversees field inquiry and fossil-energy extraction. The Vanga Maritime Federation inherits maritime commercial practices from former Tuli pearl-divers and delta merchants. The Vayu and Dwarkadhish Coral Monarchies preserve coastal gate and conch trade interests. The Shwetavahana Riverine League controls banks, locks and tolls through its Silvershore inheritance. The Kingdom of Pragjyotisha is recorded as a Harappan remnant polity, but its relation to the Union remains open. The Realm of Prince Vijaya has no assigned historical people in the current faction register.

Each survives in the same age without being forced into a common ancestral succession from Sudās's battlefield.

## IV. A university within the inheritance
The University should build a three-level collection:
1. **Older witness:** song, testimony, court list, findspot, material culture.
2. **Civic successor:** tablet, seal, deed, apprenticeship register, tax dispute, municipal order.
3. **Industrial successor:** patent, survey, rail charter, guild ledger, correspondence, excavation record.

An accepted historical connection requires a defensible path across these levels. Two similar names centuries apart are a research lead, not a conclusion.

## V. What survived and what changed
The Age of Machinery should show both preservation and transformation. A guide's obligation might become a railway escort statute; the obligation to return a lost animal might become insured cargo liability; a household hearth custodian might become a regulated furnace examiner. But each transformation may also have independent origins. The university must distinguish close analogy from evidenced descent.

And some customs may end entirely. The absence of a modern successor is not proof that a culture failed.

## VI. The historical horizon
By the late industrial age, Narmada excavations approach the dangerous boundary of the Great Shattering. The pre-Shattering chart locates the relevant nations; it does not determine the cause of their later destruction or the exact fate of every ancient inheritance.

The completed chronicle should close **before** the Shattering, with the plural industrial world already established. Its final question is how institutions remembered older peoples, not whether a single triumphant tribe became the machinery age.
