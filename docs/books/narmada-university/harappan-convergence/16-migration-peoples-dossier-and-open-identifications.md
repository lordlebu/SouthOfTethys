*Names that may designate peoples, localities, alliances, epithets or beings*

## Reading a name
The University records the comparative poetic evidence before proposing a South of Tethys locality. A hostile epithet cannot become an official tribal census entry. A named people does not automatically possess one capital, a hereditary dynasty or a uniform fate.

## The problem of self-designation
A poem may name an opponent without preserving what that opponent called itself. **No self-designation has been verified in the current South of Tethys archive for the newly proposed Anu, Druhyu, Pūru, Bharata, Tṛtsu, Paktha, Bhalāna, Alina or Viṣāṇin populations.** Their names remain comparative labels, not established local census records.

Dasyu cannot serve as the name of a conquered ethnicity merely because a victorious speaker used it. The Paṇi dialogue does not by itself prove that all wealthy merchants belonged to one people. Ahi remains distinct from human political populations.

## Independent localities
Paktha, Bhalāna, Alina, Śiva and Viṣāṇin are entered as **unlocated names**, not silently assigned to known mountains or valleys. The University must locate a dated local witness, establish whether the term was a self-name, and distinguish a travelling contingent from a permanent settlement before publishing a map.

## The two Kashyapa questions
The Vedda Kashyapa is established as a remote oral origin-name. A Kāśyapa attribution in a comparative hymn register may belong to a singer, family or teaching lineage. The archive does not demonstrate identity. These entries must remain separate even if their spelling is later normalized.

## What the register does establish
The Rigvedic comparative material supplies a vocabulary of rival peoples and political labels suitable for investigating the later migration horizon. The structured South of Tethys archive supplies its own Vedda, Naga, Harappan, Kia and Mask Family histories. The two sets of evidence may be compared, but resemblance alone does not authorize merger.

## Outstanding verification
Exact hymn attributions and variant orthography require an edition-level philological audit. Until that work is complete, no disputed name receives a map pin, hereditary origin or primary-canon status. This is a completed classification register with **explicit unresolved identifications**, not a claim that the unresolved names have been independently attested.
