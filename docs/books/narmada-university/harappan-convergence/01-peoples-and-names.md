# Peoples, Names and Political Labels
*The provisional ethnographic register of the later migration horizon*

## The problem of names
A poem is not a census. Some names denote confederations, others patrons or armies, and still others are the language used by rivals for people they disliked. The University therefore records **what kind of identification the name permits** before assigning a territory.

| Name | Working historical reading | Entry into the Harappan record |
|---|---|---|
| **Anu** | Named political people or association | Possible treaty and settlement communities; location not established |
| **Druhyu** | Named people associated with conflicts | Possible caravan and frontier successors, unverified |
| **Pūru** | Major named people and political grouping | Possible independent partner, rival or urban constituency; no single destiny assigned |
| **Bharata** | Naga people; also linked in song to Sudās | Hastinapura settlement at the opening of Civilization Dawn; precise court relationship unresolved |
| **Tṛtsu** | Group closely associated with Sudās's side | Candidate court and fighting constituency; relation to Bharata requires careful dating |
| **Yadu** | Mixed Vedda–Naga–Harappan people | Founders of North Dwarka; coastal hegemony before Lothal's rise; Mask Family connection |
| **Turvaśa** | Named people, sometimes coupled with Yadu | Independent dossier; no assumed single homeland |
| **Bhṛgu** | Named ritual lineage and wartime grouping | Fire schools, craft patronage and disputed war allegiances |
| **Paktha, Bhalāna, Alina** | Names in the war account | Possible hill, pass or alliance constituencies, not mapped |
| **Śiva, Viṣāṇin, Matsya** | Names or designations in the conflict cycle | Names remain open; not assigned to current species, regions or dynasties |
| **Paṇi** | Adversarial possessors or keepers of wealth in songs | A possible commercial epithet; **not yet an established ethnic tribe** |
| **Dasyu** | Hostile, context-dependent designation | Record *who called whom Dasyu*; never an official ethnonym without self-identification |
| **Ahi** | Adversarial being or epithet in Indr's songs | An entry in the register of beings and poetic designations, **not a human tribe** |

The form **Parni** must not be silently collapsed with **Paṇi**. The scribal evidence for a distinct Parni people has not yet been established in the present comparison.

## The newcomers and the older populations
The Vedda and Naga histories already exist independently of these entries. No Anu, Pūru, Bharata or Bhṛgu family is retrospectively declared the founder of Vedda or Naga society. A name may enter a Naga court as the title of a visitor, a contracted translator or a hostile report without changing the court's ancestry.

Similarly, the Kia marsh communities and Harappan stone-engineers cannot be reassigned to a newly named ancient tribe merely because their crafts resemble descriptions in a song.

## Four mechanisms of merging
**Contractual entry.** A family or caravan takes an oath, registers a claim, pays transit dues or becomes a recognized guest of a city.

**Occupational entry.** Makers, recorders, fire custodians, guides and merchants enter wards or guilds. Techniques pass between unrelated teachers and apprentices.

**Political entry.** Defeated soldiers or former allies receive asylum, garrison rights or marriage settlements. A local ruler may remain autonomous under a trade or security compact.

**Cultural transmission.** A song, ritual gesture, agricultural tool or measurement method survives in a new language. The old ethnonym may be forgotten even where the practice endures.

## Register precautions
Do not equate a hostile name with a biological people. Do not create a separate kingdom for every name appearing in a victory hymn. Do not infer ancestry from modern political borders. Do not treat a ruler's praise-poem as a neutral account of his enemies.

For each accepted people, the University will eventually require: earliest witness; alternative spellings; self-name versus outside name; place evidence; political alliances; contacts with existing peoples; shifts in residence; and any traceable later institutional survival.
