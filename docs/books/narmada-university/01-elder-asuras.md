# The Elder Asuras: Sovereignty Before the Gates

*Studies in Ancient Testimony, Volume I*  
*Narmada University — Collegium of Historical Inquiry*

## Preface
The word *Elder* is an archival convenience, not evidence that all bearers of the Asura name shared a dynasty. In the oldest surviving traditions, the Asuras are remembered through order, sovereign authority, disputed wisdom, and intrusions from beyond the charted world. These testimonies must not be forced into a single genealogy.

## I. Varuṇa, Asura Mahat
The designation **Asura Mahat** is preserved for Varuṇa of the Deep, Keeper of Ṛta. His guardianship of cosmic order and the ancient Tethys does not make him a member of the later Maya-born courts. The classification of Varuṇa as celestial deity remains distinct from the classifications of those who arrive through gates. The title is a theological and historical problem, not a licence to redraw his nature.

## II. Vairochana and the Elder Name
Vairochana appears in Elder catalogues as an Asura whose sovereignty is remembered more clearly than its geographical seat. Competing schools disagree whether the title indicates an individual ruler, a tradition of instruction, or an authority subsequently claimed by others. None has produced a secure route from his court to the settlements of later Jambhudweepa.

## III. The House Catalogues
Accounts of Hiraṇyakaśipu, Hiraṇyākṣa, Prahlāda, Mahābali and Maya Dānava circulate among the antiquarians. They are transmitted as inherited traditions, not certified biographies of inhabitants of South of Tethys. No epic hero is thereby imported into the University's contemporary history. A name preserved in a catalogue is not a witnessed visitor.

## IV. The Country Beyond the Wall
The earliest witnesses speak of arrivals from beyond the Ice Wall, while later commentators invoke rifts and a designation called *Asura Loka*. No survey has demonstrated that this latter phrase identifies a physical homeland. It may refer to a realm, a remembered direction, or an interpretation of the unknowable.

## V. Inheritance Without Blood
Later Asuras may inherit techniques, institutions, forbidden records or titles. The claim of inheritance is not proof of biological descent from the Elders. This distinction governs the volumes that follow.

## Closing Judgment
We preserve the Elder names because they illuminate the language of power. We refuse to turn their preservation into an invented family tree.
