# Manjalaya: The Princess of Shadows and the Broken Passage

*Studies in Ancient Testimony, Volume V*  
*Narmada University — Department of Royal Traditions*

## Preface
Manjalaya, called Vishaili, is remembered as an Asura princess of shadows. The chronicles place her summoning in late Deep Antiquity, but their later accounts extend beyond the securely dated chapters.

## I. The Shadow Pact
Varunesh is said to have performed a dark rite involving the sacrifice of an Indus unicorn at a Dwarka Gate. Manjalaya's arrival is central to the Shadow Pact. The structured records name the Ancient Courts as the event's location, while the narrative names a gate; these descriptions are not yet geographically reconciled.

## II. Union and Diminishment
One account holds that her relationship with Varunesh gradually diminished her powers and near-immortality. The mechanism is unknown. It would be improper to transform this account into a general law governing every Asura.

## III. The Daughter
The chronicles preserve the existence of an Asura-Tainted Princess. Her later life belongs to its own protected record and is not reconstructed here.

## IV. A Witness in the Wilderness
Sarishne, a Rakshasa associated with the Tenduas, secretly witnessed the original pact. A distinct, later migration-era disturbance brought her into contact with Manjalaya again. Their association establishes historical contact across lineages, not common ancestry.

## V. The Unsettled Last Account
A later tradition places Sarishne and her Oni partner Gagdji at Manjalaya's peaceful death. Its date and chapter placement are provisional. The University records the account without assigning a year or declaring that all chronologies have been reconciled.

## Closing Judgment
Manjalaya's history concerns the limits of passage, power and testimony. It is not a warrant to merge Rakshasa, Oni and Asura identities.
