# The Ancestral Transmission Atlas

*Studies in Ancient Testimony — Narmada University — editorial review*

## Method
This is the fictional history of **South of Tethys, not Hindu mythology**. **Nagas are non-serpentine peoples.** Names that resemble outside traditions do not transfer their biographies, iconography, anatomy, genealogies or cosmology. The ancestral traditions originated among distinct migrating populations and interwove after arrival on Jambhudweepa. Later cultural custody is not blood descent.

**Evidence labels:** Existing canon (structured repository history); attributed tradition (in-world testimony); proposed reconstruction (new editorial hypothesis); unresolved (no defensible determination).

## Five collections
- **Founders and the Common Hearth:** Ilā, Vivasvān, Vāc, Tvaṣṭṛ, Vaiśvānara.
- **Houses of Passage:** Aryaman, Bhaga, Savitṛ, Pūṣan, Wiznu the Strider, Vāta.
- **Waters and the Wanderers:** Āpas, Apām Napāt, Ṛbhus, Nāsatyas.
- **Boundaries, Loss and Renewal:** Nirṛti, Manyu, Rākā, Kuhū, Prajāpati, Skambha.
- **Celestial Appeals and Wilderness Traditions:** Indr, Varuṇa, Mitra, Rudra, Maruts, Dyauṣ.

## Anomaly resolution
**Wiznu** investigates anomalous places and attempts to dispel suspected Asura influence; **Aryaman** gathers witnesses and consent; **Vaiśvānara** is linked to the common protective fire; **Skambha** traditions consider unusual foundations. Wiznu and Aryaman **attempted to invoke Indr**, the existing celestial Thunder-Bearer. The result is unknown: no verified appearance or intervention is asserted. Not every anomaly is caused by Asuras.

## Lost knowledge
Vāc preserves oral testimony, Tvaṣṭṛ's makers preserve practical craft, and Ṛbhus traditions attempt reconstruction. Pashupati's pre-Jharwa court possessed nature-bound rites learned through contact with intelligent surviving Vanaras. It predates major Jharwa migrations; its ritual competence declined by generational knowledge loss. Later communities may inherit fragments without founding that court or becoming its descendants.

## Settlement and recovery
Ilā's household inclusion, Bhaga's allocation, Aryaman's hospitality, Mitra's oath traditions and Rākā's communal reconciliation may overlap among later Jharwa and Harappan communities. These are proposed connections, not exclusive tribal religions.

## Frontier practice
Pūṣan's guides, Vāta's weather readers, Rudra's wilderness healers, Maruts' storm witnesses and Nāsatyas' rescue traditions can be adopted by Vedda and other communities without assigning a single ancestry.

## Waters and Naga peoples
Āpas and Apām Napāt traditions may be investigated in Naga, riverine, coastal and Dwarka contexts. **Nagas are people, not snakes**; no serpent body, snake cult or external mythic genealogy is implied.

## The Aravali Alms Step
**Existing canon:** The surviving domed temple and inscribed plinth commemorate Jharwa dead from the Aravali massacre. The temple was raised by descendants of those responsible. **Proposed earlier attribution:** Wiznu and Aryaman's followers may have maintained protective observances at the crossing. The existence of an earlier building on the exact footprint is unresolved. Other claimed foundations remain unnamed and unlocated.

## Ontological limits
Indr, Varuṇa, Mitra and Dyauṣ retain existing celestial identities. Varuṇa's Asura Mahat title does not make him a Maya-born Asura. Mortal Mithra is not silently equated with celestial Mitra. Ṛbhus and Maruts can be collective traditions. No verified physical Asura Loka is invented.

## Review questions
Which later custodians possess traceable testimony? Which sites show genuine earlier phases? Where did a ritual become a political claim? Which anomalies have mundane explanations? Which names identify people, offices, collectives or invocations of existing deities?

**Scope:** Narmada University manuscripts only. No structured canon, timeline, location, game or deployment modifications.
