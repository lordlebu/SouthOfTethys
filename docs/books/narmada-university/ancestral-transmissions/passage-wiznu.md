# Wiznu the Strider: Testimony and Later Inheritance

*Studies in Ancient Testimony — Houses of Passage*  
*Narmada University, Collegium of Historical Inquiry*

## I. The Name and Its Witnesses
**Working classification:** remembered anomaly investigator; individual or transmitted office. The University's use of this name is not proof of a single historical person. Where an office, collective or celestial entity is possible, those possibilities remain distinct.

## II. Historical Appearance and Disposition
Long-legged, broad-backed traveller with a small scar beneath one eye; fearless in inquiry, sometimes dangerously unwilling to retreat. This is an illustrative historical portrait, not an excavated likeness. Distinct communities may have pictured the bearer differently.

## III. The Earlier Tradition
The tradition is not assigned a shared original homeland with other ancestral names. Accounts that place all figures in one founding council are late attempts to reconcile traditions that travelled separately. The earliest dates, migration routes and original witnesses remain unresolved.

## IV. The Post-Arrival Problem
With Aryaman attempted to invoke Indr; Vaiśvānara's fire is associated with the observance. The dispute matters because later institutions could inherit the practice while disagreeing about its moral meaning, supernatural efficacy or right of custody. A rite's survival is not evidence that its original explanation was correct.

## V. Later Peoples and Institutions
**Communities for investigation:** Aravali crossing traditions, Jharwa and Naga site custodians. **Characteristic inheritance:** veneration, investigation and dispelling suspected Asura influence. These are proposed transmission paths rather than established exclusive tribal cults. Naga communities are considered as historical custodians where testimony supports that association.

## VI. Competing Testimonies
A custodian's account treats the practice as a genuine inheritance; a neighbouring account may describe borrowing, appropriation or independent discovery. The University records both without manufacturing blood descent. Connections with the Jharwa, Vedda, Naga and Harappan histories require corroboration in this setting rather than analogy to outside mythology.

## The Indr appeal and the Aravali
Wiznu and Aryaman attempted to invoke the already-celestial Indr during an exceptional disturbance; the rite's result is unresolved. Vaiśvānara's common flame is associated with their protective observances. The surviving Alms Step temple is **not** their construction: its existing canon associates the later domed memorial and names on its plinth with the Jharwa dead of the Aravali massacre and descendants of those responsible. An earlier Wiznu–Aryaman observance at the crossing is a proposed attribution, not a proven building phase. Other sacred foundations remain unnamed pending evidence.

## VII. Closing Judgment and Evidence Register
**Existing canon:** Broad migration sequence, distinct later populations and already-established celestial identities where applicable. **Proposed:** Portraits of mortal bearers, later custodians, practice-specialization and specific disputes. **Unresolved:** original homeland, chronology, ancestry, actual ritual efficacy and the identity of historical officeholders. No character JSON, event, map, place, game or other structured canon is changed.
