# Ṛbhus: Testimony and Later Inheritance

*Studies in Ancient Testimony — Waters and the Wanderers*  
*Narmada University, Collegium of Historical Inquiry*

## I. The Name and Its Witnesses
**Working classification:** collective of skilled makers, not necessarily brothers. The University's use of this name is not proof of a single historical person. Where an office, collective or celestial entity is possible, those possibilities remain distinct.

## II. Historical Appearance and Disposition
Illustrative members: three differently aged makers with clay-dusted hands; no uniform faces or shared ancestry presumed. This is an illustrative historical portrait, not an excavated likeness. Distinct communities may have pictured the bearer differently.

## III. The Earlier Tradition
The tradition is not assigned a shared original homeland with other ancestral names. Accounts that place all figures in one founding council are late attempts to reconcile traditions that travelled separately. The earliest dates, migration routes and original witnesses remain unresolved.

## IV. The Post-Arrival Problem
A repaired object is credited to miracles by some and apprenticeship by others. The dispute matters because later institutions could inherit the practice while disagreeing about its moral meaning, supernatural efficacy or right of custody. A rite's survival is not evidence that its original explanation was correct.

## V. Later Peoples and Institutions
**Communities for investigation:** Harappan technical houses and Tvaṣṭṛ-linked guilds. **Characteristic inheritance:** repair, reproduction and recovery of lost techniques. These are proposed transmission paths rather than established exclusive tribal cults. Naga communities are considered as historical custodians where testimony supports that association.

## VI. Competing Testimonies
A custodian's account treats the practice as a genuine inheritance; a neighbouring account may describe borrowing, appropriation or independent discovery. The University records both without manufacturing blood descent. Connections with the Jharwa, Vedda, Naga and Harappan histories require corroboration in this setting rather than analogy to outside mythology.

## VII. Closing Judgment and Evidence Register
**Existing canon:** Broad migration sequence, distinct later populations and already-established celestial identities where applicable. **Proposed:** Portraits of mortal bearers, later custodians, practice-specialization and specific disputes. **Unresolved:** original homeland, chronology, ancestry, actual ritual efficacy and the identity of historical officeholders. No character JSON, event, map, place, game or other structured canon is changed.
