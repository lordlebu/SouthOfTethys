# Ethnicity, Constitutions and the National Awakenings
*Studies of the transformation from dynastic subjects to citizens*

## The difference between a kingdom and a nation
A ruler's realm may include several peoples without creating a single common national identity. Conversely, speakers of a related tongue may serve rival crowns. The University reconstructs nationalism only when an institution makes the claim: a newspaper, provincial assembly, political club, army oath, school register, petition, revolt, or electoral party. A sacred figure's name alone is insufficient evidence.

## Nine proposed movements, none automatically separatist
| Organisation or tradition (proposed) | Grievance and social base | Constitutional demand | Cross-era consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kia Water Rights League | marsh families excluded by Lothal canal taxes | water-council seats and wetland jurisdiction | a federated part of the Industrial Union |
| Jangubai Workers' Houses | mine apprentices and debt-bound labour | independent lodging, contracts and injury relief | supports Narmada charter republicanism |
| Saljong Grain Movement | inland seed households versus export merchants | guaranteed local reserves and elected port inspectors | federal powers within Vanga |
| Stone Assembly Movement | eastern hill constituencies threatened by valley officials | hill legislative councils, shared road consent | Pragjyotisha's autonomous districts |
| Nine-Pass League | pastoral clans displaced by railway rights-of-way | separate pasture, transit and rail ownership | joint rail-khanate constitution |
| Niyam Watershed League | upland cultivators and miners at odds with concessions | binding local water and quarry review | charter provinces in the southern realm |
| Tökisy Pasture Association | cattle and buffalo herders divided by freight routes | seasonal crossing easements | pastoral canton autonomy |
| Grove Covenant League | forest and hill households facing timber charters | protected commons, community jurisdiction | rival statutes across several states |
| Coral Custodialists | coastal lineages fearing private exploitation of sea gates | hereditary guardianship under public review | constitutional survival of dual monarchies |

These are **proposed historical associations**, not canon parties. Their constituencies need not be ethnically uniform and must not be assigned to real present-day peoples simply by a parallel religious name.

## The census problem
Officials have motives to make clear categories: tax liability, land holding, language schooling, conscription. Villagers have motives to resist: a label may erase a marriage network, common land or local privilege. To narrate an ethnic awakening, the University should compare self-names, rulers' names, outsiders' categories and changes in official usage. A hostile designation must not be promoted to an ethnic fact.

## The bargain that holds states together
National integration can arise through a universal tax court, common protection against piracy, grain reserves, a unified rail gauge or recognition of minority schools. It can also fail through forced assimilation or extraction. The most convincing modern national history contains a failed centralising scheme, a named political opposition and a constitutional compromise or unresolved conflict.

## External methodological analogies
For scholarly comparison rather than imported events: Charles Tilly's account of coercion, taxation and capital; research on Asian state formation by emulation and kinship; and scholarship on territorial autonomy and multi-layered ethnicity. These are models of causation, **not** evidence that Jambhudweepa experienced a real-world colonial chronology or a particular modern nationalist movement.
