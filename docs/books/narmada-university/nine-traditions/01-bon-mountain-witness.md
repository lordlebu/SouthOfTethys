# Tonpa Shenrab: The Mountain Witness
*The first register — a human teacher and the contested high-pass archives*

A caravan's winter record preserves the name **Tonpa Shenrab** among those who taught strangers how to cross a dangerous upland route. In this reconstruction he is **a mortal guide and teacher of memory**, not a celestial founder. The title *Tonpa* appears in more than one late copy, and the University cannot decide whether all copies describe the same traveler.

## Civilization Dawn
One testimony tells of Shenrab mediating between a highland winter camp and merchants bound for river towns. He refuses to surrender the location of sheltered springs to a passing armed escort unless safe passage is extended to the camp's children and animals. His achievement is an agreement and a set of memorized routes, not a miraculous mountain opening.

The Harappan and Vedda merchant parties that later repeat the account would each have reasons to alter it. In one version the guide saves traders; in another he prevents traders from claiming winter stores. Both versions survive.

## The long interval
The routes persist in work songs and directional cairns. Generations later, a teacher may be called Shenrab without kinship to the first. Copies appear in several languages with incompatible descriptions of the same pass. The University refuses to choose one location without field evidence.

## Age of Machinery
Engineers surveying a northern rail corridor find a cairn sequence used by local guides. The **Hyrkanian and Tushara Rail-Khanates** value its accuracy; a Narmada surveyor points out that the old compact protected springs, not merely the path through them. A machine-age debate follows: whether a survey can convert inherited passage rights into exclusive rights to build a line.

The continuity proposed here is **cartographic knowledge and a contested public obligation**, not divine descent.
