# Niyam Raja and Dharani Penu: The Watershed Pact
*The ninth register — a guardian of uplands and a keeper of earthwork obligations*

**Niyam Raja** appears in upland testimony as a mountain guardian or ruler; **Dharani Penu** as the earth's custodian in later retelling. The University's **invented mortal-person hypothesis** regards these as names borne by a hill negotiator and a land steward. This is not a real-world origin story for Dongria Kondh or other Kondh beliefs.

## Civilization Dawn
River settlements request stone from an upland quarry. Niyam Raja, named by some witnesses as a person and others as a title, permits only limited working above a known spring. Dharani Penu insists that debris must be returned to the worked slope, keeping runoff away from the fields below. The agreement is witnessed by hill residents and lowland traders; none controls all of Jambhudweepa.

## Conflicting inheritances
Quarry records celebrate the right to extract. Highland testimony remembers the right to stop. Later copies change the names' grammatical form, and some commentators treat them as eternal rather than human. There is no surviving continuous line of custody.

## Age of Machinery
Geological surveyors discover the same rock band that feeds an industrial mineral corridor. A mining consortium cites the old right; engineers find that broken slopes threaten both the spring and a nearby mechanical canal. The Narmada University file closes on a question rather than a verdict: **can a concession endure after the obligations that justified it have been forgotten?**
