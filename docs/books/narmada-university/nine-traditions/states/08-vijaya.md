# Vijaya: The Southern Dynasty and Its Treaty States
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchor.** Prince Vijaya's southern political ambitions and contact with Lothal during Civilization Dawn precede a later Realm of Prince Vijaya. The exact genealogy and intervening acquisitions are not yet established.

### I. A frontier prince chooses the south
In the proposed history, Vijaya's failure to dominate Lothal redirects expansion toward Tamralinga-linked ports, island alliances and southern trade. He develops dynastic legitimacy through protection of harbours, fortified warehouses and negotiated marriage, not a blanket conquest of every southern people.

### II. The Nilgiri bargain
Earlier unnamed pastoral councils negotiate seasonal access with the crown.

### III. The watershed principalities
Older upland coalitions retain customary watershed rights.

### IV. A plural crown
A Vijayan realm forms from crown provinces, treaty highlands, self-governing ports and protected uplands. Its subjects are not all one ethnicity. Later school and military reforms promote an umbrella Vijayan nationality, while pastoral and watershed assemblies form competing political movements.

### V. Machinery crisis
Rail corridors and mines require standardized rights-of-way, yet old treaties forbid arbitrary royal enclosure. Constitutional reform either converts the crown into a federation of provinces or risks provincial revolt. The state therefore persists as a **realm**, not a simple replicated industrial republic.

### Early Machinery political actors (around or after Year 2,200)
Tökisy personally leads pastoral autonomy negotiations in Early Machinery; Niyam Raja is a contemporary highland monarch and Dharani Penu a contemporary agrarian rights leader. Their High Pasture Treaty and Watershed Compact address industrial rail and mining concessions. These are mortal individuals, not ancient namesakes; earlier institutions predate them.

**Tests:** dynasty genealogy, existing Tamralinga and Vijaya canon, actual highland geography and whether the negotiated polities coincide with any established nations.

The Shared Pasture Agreement records Tökisy; the Quarry Pact records Niyam Raja and Dharani Penu. The High Pasture Treaty and Watershed Compact have empty participant lists; their involvement remains proposed. See [Register of Persons and Public Acts](../14-character-event-register.md).
