# Pragjyotisha: A Composite Eastern Kingdom
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchor.** The Age of Machinery includes a Kingdom of Pragjyotisha. Its earlier dynastic sequence and constituent hill jurisdictions require reconstruction rather than retrospective invention as canon.

### I. The valley crown
Valley courts consolidate irrigation, fortified markets and military obligations. In the proposed reconstruction, **Donyi** and **Polo** are two historical court astronomers or offices whose rival calendars become the **Twin Reckoning**, a common seasonal standard that still lets valleys preserve local time. The real Donyi-Polo cosmology is not claimed to derive from these fictional humans.

### II. Upland autonomy
A separate hill tradition remembers **U Blei Nongthaw** and **U Ryngkew** as builder, witness, or honorary office-bearers behind a stone-marked assembly. They symbolize an already-existing autonomous polity, not the foundation of every Khasi community. **Singbonga, Marang Buru and Jaher Era** remain separate grove/hill witnesses with no invented common ancestry or royal marriage. The crown may receive forest treaties from different communities without homogenizing them.

### III. Crowns versus council stones
Kingdom-wide taxation threatens pasture and spring rights. The proposed **Stone Assembly Movement** demands consent for roads, levies and military recruitment. A royal autonomy charter grants legislative standing to hill councils while reserving defence and external diplomacy to the crown.

### IV. Machinery and nationality
Rail engineers and census clerks urge a common language and uniform survey. Hill politicians offer a different national bargain: loyalty to Pragjyotisha in exchange for provincial languages, communal land protections and assembly vetoes. The kingdom survives because composite sovereignty is a workable constitutional settlement, not because ethnicity has vanished.

**Tests:** any existing mythology attached to Pragjyotisha, its geographical limits, the legal status of named hills and documented royal succession.
