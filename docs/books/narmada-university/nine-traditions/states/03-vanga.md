# Vanga: Harbour Confederacy to Maritime Federation
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchor.** Tuli-linked seafaring and eastern harbour communities precede the later Vanga Maritime Federation, whose naval transport and overseas trade do not imply an originally unified monarchy.

### I. The harvest crisis
The proposed leaders **Tatara-Rabuga** and **Saljong** operate across different communities: a hill allocation chief and a grain organiser whose boats carry planting seed after scarcity. Their remembered **Seed Fleet** is a political coalition to keep harbours from starving each other, not the foundation of real Songsarek belief.

### II. First admiralty
Cyclone losses, piracy and mutually hostile customs houses push ports toward joint patrols and common salvage courts. A harbour retains its assembly yet owes ships and supplies to the league. This makes a federation structurally plausible without one conqueror claiming the whole coast.

### III. Merchant oligarchy versus federation
The rise of large warehouses and external commercial loans gives shipowners disproportionate power. Inland seed communities and fishers form the proposed **Saljong Grain Movement** and demand equal emergency access to stores. After civil unrest, a Federal Harbour Charter separates admiralty, customs and grain reserves.

### IV. Industrial Vanga
Steamships reward large capital, but individual port laws survive. Dockworkers and pearl-diving households contest whether shareholding alone grants national representation. Vanga identifies as a maritime federation of unequal members rather than a uniformly administered coastal empire.

**Tests:** which Tuli settlements entered first, the chronological relationship of eastern river polities, port charter texts and realistic limits of navigable trade routes.
