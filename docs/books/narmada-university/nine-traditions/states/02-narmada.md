# Narmada: The Crown That Became an Academic Republic
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchor.** The Age of Machinery's Narmada Academic Republic is an intellectual and surveying state; the earlier plateau populations and Harappan frontier histories have their own origins.

### I. The Furnace Confederacy
The proposed **Furnace Confederacy** arises when plateau chiefs require common ore weights and security for furnace paths. Older furnace councils predate the named industrial leaders. The database places the Formation of the Furnace Confederacy in the early industrial transformation; its precise epoch remains unset.

### II. From clan tribute to the Narmada Crown
A dominant household converts furnace dues into garrison pay and buys safe river passage. Yet forest assemblies, mining towns and teaching houses retain separate charters. The crown's territory is never the same as the extent of all its ore contracts.

### III. The Charter Crisis
A monarch attempts to monopolise mineral titles and revoke scholarly exemptions. University jurists, mining councils, debtors and city militias oppose the crown. In the proposed **Plateau Charter Crisis**, the state splits; a negotiated abdication or exile makes room for a constitutional republic. Which outcome occurred should remain uncertain until chronology is fixed.

### IV. The republic's national project
Surveying, archival law and civil-service examination acquire sovereign weight because the republic's authority depends on enforceable written charters. The Jangubai Workers' Houses resist industrial debt coercion. Grove and watershed communities challenge the university's temptation to mistake a map for an ownership deed. The republic is not politically homogeneous merely because its university has a common curriculum.

### Early Machinery political actors (around or after Year 2,200)
Bada Deo organizes a new industrial confederation, Kupar Lingo leads cultural and craft reform, and Jangubai personally leads the workers' movement during Early Machinery. These are mortal individuals, not ancient namesakes; earlier institutions predate them.

**Tests:** surviving royal seals, competing charters, forest-canton permissions and a chain of institutional transmission between the Confederacy and the machine-era university.
