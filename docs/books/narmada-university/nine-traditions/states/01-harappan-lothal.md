# Harappan–Lothal: From the Mask Crown to the Industrial Union
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchors.** Civilization Dawn Lothal, the Mask Family, Kia alliance and Tendua Crisis precede the Age of Machinery. The later Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union is a Lothal-centred polity with foundries, guilds, patent registers and civic councils. No new story here displaces those events.

### I. City sovereignty
The Mask ruler's household claims the wall, harbour tax and grain stores. Kia river councils retain water rights, fisheries and navigable channels even where city officials assert a larger territorial mandate. A proposed **Canal Compact** negotiates rather than abolishes this duality. The early compact predates the three named reformers.

### II. Competition among estuaries
In the proposed **War of the Three Estuaries**, Lothal seeks toll control over rival ports. Victory requires shipbuilding credit and marsh pilots; royal guarantees to funders empower hereditary guild chambers. Harappan masonry, Kia hydraulic knowledge and independent local crafts coexist rather than deriving from one founder.

### III. Succession and fiscal crisis
A succession dispute prevents the crown from meeting war debt. Ironmasters, shipping houses, Kia assemblies and municipal officials negotiate a **Guild Settlement**: the dynasty loses exclusive executive control, and guild and water representatives share jurisdiction. This explains how a city-state can become an industrial *union*, not merely a larger city with machines.

### IV. Machinery and national identity
Steam pumping centralises drainage, and patent registration shifts bargaining from workshops to state tribunals. The Kia Water Rights League fights for protected wetland seats; labourers seek voting rules not tied to guild wealth. Citizens may be Kia by family, Lothali by residence and Union nationals by political allegiance. The unresolved issue is who constitutes the industrial public.

### Early Machinery political actors (around or after Year 2,200)
Pakhangba, Sanamahi and Leimarel Sidabi are contemporary Early Machinery monarch, constitutional reformer and queen-administrator respectively. They renegotiate older canal rights during the Guild Settlement and industrial drainage crisis. These are mortal individuals, not ancient namesakes; earlier institutions predate them.
