# Shwetavahana: From River Dynasty to Navigation League
*Political dossier — reconstruction for review*

**Attested anchor.** The Shwetavahana/Silvershore dynastic tradition concerns river lordship, silver, debt and commerce; the later Shwetavahana Riverine League operates river trade, locks and banking.

### I. A dynasty of toll stations
Shwetavahana princes initially command crossings and boat escorts. Marriage alliances install junior branches at tributaries. Their control resembles an archipelago of fortified customs houses, not a modern unbroken territory.

### II. Money travels farther than armies
Royal credit instruments become familiar to merchants who never swear personal allegiance to the king. River towns form navigation assemblies to maintain locks, towpaths and standardized coinage. Conflicts arise when a prince's soldiers extract tolls from barges carrying the prince's own creditors' loans.

### III. The Argenthal Settlement
In the **proposed Argenthal Settlement**, insolvent provincial princes concede navigation law and treasury audit to a council of creditors and chartered towns. The senior dynasty remains as river patron, its succession ceremonies separated from the management of state loans.

### IV. Machinery
Steam locks and floating markets give the league practical powers across several jurisdictions. The Navigation Party demands a common schedule and currency; boatmen's associations resist dues imposed without representation. Shwetavahana now denotes dynasty, riverine nationality and contractual citizenship, not necessarily one ethnicity.

**Tests:** the actual royal genealogies, the position of Silvershore in the atlas and evidence for the league's limits on land beyond the banks.
