# U Blei Nongthaw and U Ryngkew: The Stone and the Assembly
*The sixth register — a remembered founder and guardian of a hill settlement*

A fragmentary ridge chronicle calls **U Blei Nongthaw** the maker of the first public meeting-place and **U Ryngkew** its later guardian. The University's humanized reading is tentative: *maker* could refer to a master builder, a patron, or the title of a creator in a ritual account. This fictional scenario does not reduce the real Khasi conception of deity to a mortal biography.

## Civilization Dawn
A hillside settlement is divided over a spring that runs beneath two paths. U Blei Nongthaw is remembered as the person who sets stones to mark common access instead of granting the site to either household. U Ryngkew subsequently watches the path, calls witnesses after a landslip and forbids the removal of the oldest stone until every affected family has been heard.

## Survival and uncertainty
The marker line is repaired several times. A later transcription mistakes the number of stones for the number of founding households. Other witnesses report that there was never a singular builder, only successive assemblies. The University records both possibilities.

## Age of Machinery
A mountain road survey encounters the stones while drafting rights of way for carts and telegraph poles. Modern legal practice treats a copyist's paper plan as final, but hill residents testify that the stones marked **access rather than property**. The Narmada archivist's role is to record the dispute honestly, not to invent a founding decree.
