# Bada Deo, Kupar Lingo and Jangubai: The Forge Compact
*The fifth register — authority, teaching and the shelter of apprentices*

The metallurgical records remember **Bada Deo** as an elder presiding over access to ore, **Kupar Lingo** as an itinerant teacher and **Jangubai** as a protector of traveling apprentices. These are **fictional human interpretations of names known in Gondi religious contexts**, not historical claims about the actual figures. Kupar Lingo is a culture-hero or teacher in the source tradition and must not be casually reclassified as an interchangeable god.

## Civilization Dawn
A furnace settlement has iron ore but cannot safely use it because the bellows and charcoal stores belong to rival houses. Bada Deo argues for shared furnace days. Kupar Lingo designs a repeatable measure for charcoal and ore; Jangubai offers shelter to the apprentices who would otherwise be held as debt labor by whichever house controls the kiln.

The compact is fragile. One household preserves only the elder's authority; another remembers the teacher's method; a third preserves a song about Jangubai's shelter.

## The centuries between
Metallurgical knowledge spreads by purchase, employment and imitation. There is no defensible claim that every furnace on the Narmada Plateau descended from a single founder. Apprentices sometimes keep the compact's name while changing its terms.

## Age of Machinery
A foundry syndicate in the **Harappan-Lothal Industrial Union** seeks patents on a sequence resembling the old charcoal measures. A Narmada legal historian asks whether a technical method inherited through public teaching may be enclosed as private property. Miners invoke Jangubai's story in demands for safe lodgings, although the details of the founding event remain disputed.

The machine-age legacy is a debate about **craft knowledge and labor obligations**.
