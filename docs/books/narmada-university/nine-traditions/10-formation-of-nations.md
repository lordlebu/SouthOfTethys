# From Houses to Nations: Political History of Jambhudweepa
*Studies in Ancient Testimony — the state-formation inquiry*

## The problem of the intervening centuries
Civilization Dawn supplies an already populous landscape: Harappan cities; Kia marsh communities; Vedda and Naga courts; Jharwa and Outlier migration strata; Tuli maritime societies; Shwetavahana river lordship; Prince Vijaya's southern aspirations; and northern pastoral polities. The Age of Machinery supplies guild nations, republics, federations, leagues, rail-khanates and continuing monarchies. Neither endpoint explains the transition by itself.

The University's **proposed reconstruction**, not established structured canon, is a sequence of overlapping cycles: **(1) local chiefdom and city formation; (2) fortified crown consolidation; (3) merchant and military competition; (4) chartered territories and commercial leagues; (5) reform, print and ethnic mobilisation; (6) industrial reorganisation and federated sovereignty**. A state may pass through several cycles, regress, split or be absorbed; the order is comparative, not a universal timetable.

## I. Sovereignty before nationhood
No tribe is a miniature present-day nation. A clan may owe allegiance to a person, a river god, a king, a port council and a pasture assembly without imagining these loyalties as exclusive. Control of a ford, seal-house, mine or harbour does not automatically create a contiguous sovereign territory. The Harappan settlement layer neither founded nor conquered every older community.

The state begins acquiring teeth when tribute can be counted, armed retainers regularly provisioned and rulings enforced outside a ruler's household. A city wall protects its merchants, but merchant credit can also capture the wall's governors. The tax collector, debt register and military caravan often matter more than the victory genealogy.

## II. Crowns and federations
The **Mask Family and Kia alliance** at Lothal provide a possible precedent for negotiated sovereignty, never grounds to declare Kia households Harappan by ancestry. Shwetavahana river rulers expand along toll stations, not necessarily by annexing all the fields between them. Northern khans join pastoral authority to caravan taxation; eastern valley courts must reckon with independent hill assemblies. No single empire is required to join these histories.

A proposed period of *Competing Crowns* produces dynastic marriage, garrisons, fortified customs houses and subordinate princes. Conquest may create a province; treaties may preserve a canton. The distinction is crucial when machine-age governments later claim uniformly bounded territory.

## III. The commercial transformation
In the proposed *Charter Age*, war loans and trade privileges create corporate governments: Lothal guilds, Vanga port councils, Shwetavahana navigation syndicates and Narmada university towns. A charter is not evidence of democracy. It may protect hereditary monopolies as well as market liberties. Ports can federate through common customs schedules and mutual fleets without sharing a monarch.

## IV. From subjects to political peoples
Printing, schooling, standard weights, census-taking and conscription give people new common institutions; resistance to those institutions produces new ethnic and provincial movements. An individual can be ethnically Kia, participate in an industrial guild, and identify with the Harappan-Lothal Union at once. A hill assembly can demand autonomy without seeking independence. Language, religion, kinship, caste, province and citizenship do not map neatly onto one another.

Parties and movements emerge from conflicts with a mechanism: a canal tax fuels Kia constitutionalism; mine debts fuel the Jangubai labour movement; grain exports fuel Saljong federalism; surveyed passes fuel a Nine-Pass league; forest concessions fuel grove and watershed self-government.

## V. Machines make stronger states, not always identical ones
Steam pumping, coal trains, telegraph signals, public debt, standards bureaus and industrial armies increase a capital's reach. Their political outcomes diverge. Lothal's industrial guilds take governing power; Narmada's charter institutions make a republic; Vanga's ports federalise; Shwetavahana's creditors convert dynastic prerogatives into a navigation league; Tushara and Hyrkania keep khans under joint rail law; Dwarka's houses guard a constitutional trust; Pragjyotisha preserves a negotiated crown; Vijaya's dynasty accepts treaty provinces.

## VI. Political chronology (now given a provisional planning scale)
The [Two Thousand and Two Hundred Years](13-two-thousand-two-hundred-years.md) supplies a **proposed 2,200-year relative scale** from Civilization Dawn to the threshold of Machinery; it is **not an attested calendar**. This table retains phase names rather than converting every treaty into an invented year. Crucially, **whale-blimp trade and routes to floating islands already exist during Civilization Dawn**. The intervening millennia concern expansion of aerial logistics, sky-mineral extraction and industrial production, not the first invention of flight.

## VII. The history is a contest of interpretations
A royal archivist calls integration pacification; an upland witness calls it enclosure. A guild calls hereditary privileges freedom; a debtor calls them monopoly. The University's files must carry both voices. Absence of documents does not prove absence of a polity, while a modern institution's use of an ancient title does not prove genealogical continuity.

## Source boundaries
The ancient Lothal, Kia, Vedda, Naga and Harappan evidence follows [The Chronicle of Convergence](../harappan-convergence/00-chronicle-of-convergence.md). The names of machinery-era polities are inherited from that chronicle. **All specifically named settlements, dynastic crises, compacts, parties and intermediary states developed here are proposals for editorial review.** The 2,200-year framework is a planning hypothesis, not a confirmed chronology; no canonical coordinate, population total or direct modern ethnic equivalence is asserted.


## Character epoch correction
All nineteen individuals in the Nine Houses register are mortal political actors of the **Early Age of Machinery**, active around or after the proposed Year 2,200 threshold. They personally participate in industrial nation-building, constitutional settlements and ethnic or labour movements. Earlier institutions and customs belong to unnamed predecessors, not these individuals. Any named movement or settlement involving them belongs to Early Machinery; earlier phases describe its institutional antecedents only. See [the character register](00-chronicle-of-nine-houses.md). Exact lifetimes remain unassigned; the proposed elapsed chronology and existing Dawn characters are unchanged.
